What this is

Hexagon (hexagonmath.org) requires depositors to declare the computational tools behind each work as free-text provider, name, and version triples. This page scans the full public catalogue through the read-only /api/v1/works endpoint and counts which LLM and AI assistants show up. Work-share means the fraction of works listing a family at least once, with \(s = 100 \cdot w / n\) and \(n = 201\). Re-run the three scripts in the three scripts in this repo to refresh every number below.

1. Coverage

135 of 201 works (67.2%) name at least one LLM or AI assistant in the structured tools field. Mining the free-text provenance statements (per-work detail records) for the same canonical families raises this to 173 works (86.1%): 38 works name a model only in prose. 64 works (31.8%) disclose no tools at all. The raw declarations use 133 distinct spellings, normalized here to 16 canonical families; version strings alone cannot be trusted (see §4).

Structured vs. prose. Structured counts come from the depositor-supplied tool triples (provider, name, version) in each work's tools field. Prose counts come from regex-mining the free-text provenance statement of each work's detail record for the same canonical families. Combined is the union of the two: a work counts once even if it names a family in both places, or twice in one. A work can therefore appear in the prose column but not the structured column when a model is mentioned only in narrative, and vice versa when a model is listed as a tool but never discussed in prose.

3. Significance

  • Two labs dominate. OpenAI ChatGPT appears on 75 works (37.3%) in the structured field and 125 combined (62.2%); OpenAI Codex on 39 structured, 47 combined; Anthropic Claude Opus on 37 structured, 56 combined. Together the two labs cover 156 of the 173 works that name any model.
  • Multi-model workflows are normal. Claude Opus and ChatGPT co-occur on 19 works; ChatGPT and Codex on 13. Depositors routinely draft with one system and code or formalize with another.
  • Lean never walks alone. 33 works list Lean or Mathlib, and 32 of them also list an LLM assistant. Machine-checked proof on Hexagon currently arrives bundled with AI assistance, not instead of it.
  • One subject hides in prose. Only 2 of 13 quantum-algebra works list an LLM in the structured field, yet 9 of them name GPT-6-Astra, OpenAI Codex, or a Danus verification system in their free-text provenance; the other 4 describe generic AI assistance. Most are quantum-cluster-algebra manuscripts from a small depositor group. Structured-only counting understates this subject; it is among the heaviest per-work AI users in the archive.
  • Disclosure is incomplete. 64 works name no tools; 38 works name a specific system only in prose. Absence from the structured tables does not mean absence of AI use — the combined 86.1% figure is still a lower bound.
  • Long tail, thin signal. Past the big three sit Bolzano (10), Harmonic Aristotle (5), Claude Code (10), Quora Poe (3, an aggregator that hides the real model), Danus (3, prose only), AxiomProver (2), then singletons: xAI Grok, Google Gemini variants, a local Qwen run, Moonshot Kimi, and one Meta Muse Spark entry. Poe-style aggregators make exact model attribution impossible.

4. Caveats

  • Declarations are depositor-supplied free text: “GPT-6 Astra”, “Astra 6”, and “gpt-6-astra (pinned …)” are one model spelled three ways, and 133 raw spellings collapse to 16 families only through the normalization table in analyze_hexagon.py, which is itself a judgment call. 44 works leave the version blank for at least one declared tool.
  • Prose mining is regex-based (PROV_PATTERNS in analyze_hexagon.py): 156 works name a specific system in prose, 11 describe AI use only generically (boilerplate declarations excluded). Every hit's matched spans are kept per work as prov_spans in the analysis JSON, so a false positive is auditable. The Danus verification system appears only in prose (3 works) and has no structured counterpart.
  • Hexagon is in public beta with 201 works at snapshot time; shares will move as the archive grows. Re-run scan_hexagon.py, analyze_hexagon.py, render_hexagon_report.py in that order.
